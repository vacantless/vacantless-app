# DESIGN - Commercial and mixed-use expansion: lease-up, marketing channels, commercial lease OCR (S576, EXPLORATION)

**Status: EXPLORATION / idea capture. Authored s576 (2026-07-26) from Noam's note. NOT a build order and NOT a design of record yet. Purpose: frame the opportunity, separate what the goal REQUIRES from what only ACCELERATES it (rule 22), and audit what already exists before anything is built. Needs Noam's shaping before it becomes a CODEX prompt.**
**Standing constraints when this does move: audit before building (most of the FEATURE rails already exist); build behind flags; land app changes natively on the Mac.**

## The opportunity (from Noam)
Some existing landlords also own small commercial or mixed-use places. Offer them commercial lease-up and marketing too: commercial listing channels (LoopNet/CoStar and Canadian ones like Spacelist), plus the same feature set we give residential (lease-up lifecycle, lease OCR, capture, tracking, notifications). Commercial leases tend to be longer and more complex, often custom and varied. Property shapes: a store with residential or commercial above, or fully commercial.

## The one strategic split that decides everything (rule 22)
Separate the FEATURES from the CHANNELS. They have opposite economics.

- **FEATURES port well and are the differentiator.** The lease-up lifecycle, lease OCR, the `/r` capture surface, per-org notifications, the tracker/verification rail, and (newly) the S575d rent-adjustment ledger are all channel-agnostic. Extending them to commercial is mostly data-model + extraction work on rails that already exist. This is where Vacantless is differentiated and where the goal is REQUIRED to go.
- **CHANNELS are a different, mostly-paid game and do NOT port cheaply.** The residential syndication engine works because Kijiji/rentals.ca/Zumper allow a free regular account to post directly, which the worker automates. The marquee commercial portals do not work that way:
  - **LoopNet** is the #1 CRE marketplace and is owned by **CoStar Group**. Listings are a paid, tiered advertising product (basic listing is heavily upsold to premium "signage" tiers), broker/landlord oriented. It is not a free direct-post channel like Kijiji.
  - **CoStar** itself is an expensive subscription research/analytics platform, the paid backbone behind LoopNet, not a place you "post to" for free.
  - **Showcase (Showcase.com)** is NOT a separate free channel. It is another CoStar Group surface in the SAME paid network as LoopNet. You reach it by paying CoStar for a listing product that distributes across LoopNet and Showcase, not via a free self-serve post. Naming caution: CoStar also launched a residential "Listing Showcase" product on the Homes.com side, which is a different thing. The one relevant here is the commercial Showcase.com within the CoStar/LoopNet network.
  - So the free-account auto-post playbook the worker relies on does NOT transfer to LoopNet/CoStar/Showcase. Treat all three as ONE later paid-tier or operator-assisted decision, not free automation.
- **The accessible commercial channels (the realistic v1 distribution set):**
  - **Spacelist (spacelist.ca)** - Canadian office/retail/industrial listing site, the closest Canadian analog to the accessible-portal model. Likely the first real commercial channel to target.
  - **ICX.ca** - the Canadian commercial counterpart to Realtor.ca (CREA's commercial exchange). Broker/MLS-gated, so it rides a licensed broker, not a free post. Name it, but it is a broker path, not self-serve.
  - **Crexi (crexi.com)** - the one marquee portal that behaves like the residential model: it lists for FREE (its wedge against LoopNet's paid tiers) and is expanding via US MLS partnerships. The catch is geography: US-centric, thin Canada inventory and traffic, so it delivers little audience for an Ontario/Windsor book today. Worth adding as a free syndication target when commercial inventory exists and max reach is wanted, but not a Canadian traffic driver and lower priority than Spacelist.
  - **Viewit.ca** - a PAID rental portal. Market context (Noam, operator knowledge): Viewit was once the DOMINANT residential rental portal, more used by landlords than anything else, and has since LOST significant overall market share, BUT it still commands a MAJOR presence in MULTI-RESIDENTIAL (apartment buildings, property-management-scale inventory). So its value today is segment-specific: it is a targeted channel for multi-res / apartment portfolios, not for scattered single units. This maps directly onto the PM-scale end of the customer base (e.g. Abbas, Agile's multi-unit), which is exactly why it is worth naming despite the overall decline. Critical audit point (rule 22): Viewit is part of the **Rentals.ca Network** (the 2021 consolidation of several Canadian rental marketplaces). We ALREADY have a proven direct-posting rentals.ca channel, so BEFORE building Viewit as a new channel or paying for it, verify whether the existing rentals.ca posting already syndicates into Viewit or shares its inventory. If it is a distinct paid surface, the multi-res segment is the reason to pay for it. Rank behind Spacelist for commercial; for residential multi-res, resolve the rentals.ca overlap first, then consider it for that segment specifically.
  - **The channels we already have that commercial can reuse today for near-zero cost:** the org's own `/r` public listing page, Facebook Page feed (facebook_feed, proven live), and Instagram (S575c, built) all work for a commercial space with no new channel integration. A retail storefront on the org's FB Page + `/r` capture is a legitimate v1 marketing surface while the portal integrations are figured out.

Implication: v1 commercial distribution is Spacelist + the existing FB/IG/`/r` rails, with LoopNet/CoStar/ICX framed as operator-assisted or paid-tier later. That keeps the first slice cheap and on existing rails, and avoids over-investing in hostile paid portals up front.

## What already exists to reuse (audit before building - rule 22)
- **Property / unit IA** (`BUILD-PROPERTIES-PERSONA-IA-S556`, the properties + units model) - the place a `use_type` and per-unit use would live. AUDIT RESULT (s576, Codex read-only): `properties.unit_type` ALREADY EXISTS but it is a RESIDENTIAL / Kijiji STRUCTURAL field (unit shape for the residential post), NOT a commercial-vs-residential use classification. Do NOT overload `unit_type` for use typing - add a SEPARATE `use_type` concept when this becomes a build. The distribution rails are reusable, but Spacelist is NOT integrated today (it is a target, not a present channel).
- **Distribution channel framework** (`distribution_channels`, `distribution_channel_accounts`, the worker mappings, the capabilities matrix) - a commercial channel is a new channel key in an existing framework, not a new framework. Spacelist would be a new mapping in the same shape as `rentalsca.json`/`zumper.json`.
- **Lease OCR rail** (`lib/lease-locator.ts` + `LEASE-OCR-EXTRACTION-SPEC-2026-07-06.md`) - the extraction pipeline exists; commercial needs a different field target, not a new pipeline.
- **The S575d rent-adjustment ledger** (`tenancy_rent_adjustments`) - already models "original plus a chain of changes over time." Commercial base-rent escalation schedules (step-ups over a 5-10 year term) map naturally onto this same append-only ledger. Strong reuse.
- **`/r` capture + notifications + tracker** - channel-agnostic, reuse as-is.

## The genuinely new work (what commercial REQUIRES that residential does not)
1. **Use typing on the property/unit model.** A NEW, SEPARATE `use_type`: residential | commercial | mixed_use, and per-unit use (a building can be retail-below + residential-above, or fully commercial). Do NOT reuse or overload the existing `properties.unit_type` (which is a residential/Kijiji structural field per the s576 audit) - that would entangle the residential post shape with use classification. Mixed-use means the type lives at the UNIT, not just the property. Residential units keep the current flow; commercial units branch to commercial fields.
2. **Commercial listing fields.** Square footage, rent as $/sqft/year (not $/month), additional rent / CAM / TMI, lease type (gross, net, triple-net/NNN), permitted use / zoning, availability date, and clear/ceiling height for industrial. The residential rent-as-monthly model does not fit.
3. **Commercial lease OCR (the hard part Noam flagged).** Commercial leases are longer, custom, and varied. Fields the residential spec does not capture: base rent + a step-up/escalation schedule, percentage rent (retail), CAM/TMI/operating-cost recovery, term + renewal option(s), tenant-improvement (TI) allowance, exclusive-use and permitted-use clauses, assignment/sublet terms, and personal/corporate guarantees. Because they are custom, extraction is lower-confidence and should lean on a confirm step (the S575d reconciliation pattern generalizes: OCR proposes, the operator/landlord confirms before anything arms). The escalation schedule feeds the same adjustment ledger.
4. **Not RTA-governed.** Residential rent-increase logic (Ontario guideline, N1) does NOT apply to commercial. Commercial rent changes follow the lease's own escalation terms. So the rent-increase engine must NOT run on commercial units. This is a hard gate, and it lines up with the S575d confirm-gate work: a commercial unit should never enter the N1/guideline path.

## A possible phasing (for discussion, not committed)
- **Phase 0 - model (dark):** add a NEW `use_type` at property + unit level (not `unit_type`); keep it model-only and dark. Commercial units, when surfaced, use existing FB/IG/`/r` marketing only. No new portal, no commercial OCR. Cheapest possible slice; proves demand. (s576 audit: Phase 0 should be model-only and dark.)
- **Phase 1 - commercial listing fields + Spacelist channel:** commercial field set, and Spacelist as the first commercial distribution channel in the existing worker framework.
- **Phase 2 - commercial lease OCR + escalation ledger:** the commercial extraction target, feeding the S575d ledger; confirm-gated; excludes commercial units from the RTA/N1 path.
- **Phase 3 - paid/broker portals:** LoopNet/CoStar as an operator-assisted or paid-tier post, ICX via a licensed broker. Only if the book justifies the cost.
